Foundation

3 Ways to Give Claude a Document

1
📋 Copy & Paste

Copy text from any PDF, Word doc, or webpage and paste it into Claude. Works for any text-based document.

2
📎 File Upload

On claude.ai, attach PDFs directly. Claude reads the file and you can ask any question about it.

3
🔗 Paste in Chunks

For very long documents, paste section by section. Ask Claude to "read and remember" each part first.

🔑
Always Tell Claude 3 Things

What the document is  ·  Who you are  ·  What you want extracted

Universal Document Prompt

My role: [e.g. I'm a PM / job applicant / team lead]
My purpose: [e.g. deciding whether to sign / briefing my team]

Important Limits

Claude is not a lawyer or accountant. For high-stakes contracts or compliance decisions, always get professional sign-off. Don't paste sensitive personal data (SSNs, bank details) into any AI tool.

Summarise vs Extract

Summarise — when you need an overview  |  Extract — when you know exactly what you're looking for

📅 Extract All Dates & Deadlines ▼
Date Extraction Prompt
Extract every date, deadline, and time-sensitive obligation.

Format as a table: Date | What Must Happen | Who Is Responsible | Consequence If Missed

If a deadline is relative (e.g. "within 30 days"), calculate the actual date.
Assuming today is [TODAY] and the contract was signed [DATE].
Bold any deadline within the next 30 days.

Risk & Obligation Extraction

Exercise 2 — Targeted Extraction

Multiple Angles, Same Document

Using the NexaCore excerpt from Exercise 1:

  • Ask: "Extract all numbers and metrics — put them in a table"
  • Ask: "Which 3 numbers matter most to a potential investor and why?"
  • Ask: "What risks does a client of NexaCore face based on this document?"

The same document gives completely different outputs depending on your question. An investor focuses on client concentration (42%) and attrition (22%). A client focuses on transformation instability and acquisition risk. This is the power of targeted extraction — not "what is this document about" but "what does this mean for me."

📝 Contract Understanding

⚖️
Not Legal Advice

Claude helps you understand contracts and ask better questions. For significant commitments, always get qualified legal advice before signing.

Plain English Translation
Contract type: [employment / vendor / service agreement]
My role: [employee / buyer / service provider]

Explain in plain English:
1. WHAT I'M AGREEING TO (my obligations, listed clearly)
2. WHAT THEY AGREE TO (their obligations)
3. MONEY (payments, penalties, fees)
4. DURATION & EXIT (how long, how to terminate)
5. WATCH OUT FOR (clauses to discuss with a lawyer)

Then: the 3 most important things to confirm before signing.

[PASTE CONTRACT]
Red Flag Scanner
My position: I am the party named as [___]

Find every clause that:
- Significantly limits my rights
- Creates disproportionate liability for me
- Is unusual or non-standard
- Could be interpreted against me
- Restricts me after this contract ends

For each red flag:
- Quote the exact clause
- Explain the risk in plain English
- Suggest what to negotiate
- Rate: 🔴 Major | 🟡 Moderate | 🟢 Minor

[PASTE CONTRACT]
Employment Contract Checklist
Check this employment contract on:

COMPENSATION
□ Salary, variable pay, review cycle
□ Bonus calculation and payment timing
□ ESOP / equity vesting terms

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
□ Who owns work I create (including personal projects?)

RESTRICTIVE COVENANTS
□ Non-compete — scope, duration, geography
□ Non-solicitation — clients and employees
□ Confidentiality — scope and duration

EXIT TERMS
□ Notice period (both directions)
□ What happens to unvested equity on exit?

Flag any clause that is non-standard or worth negotiating.

Exercise 5 — Decode These Clauses

What Do These Actually Mean?

4 Contract Clauses to Decode
CLAUSE A (IP Assignment):
"Employee assigns to the Company all Inventions conceived during employment, whether or not during working hours."

CLAUSE B (Non-Compete):
"Employee shall not engage in any business competing with the Company in any market where the Company currently operates or has expressed intention to operate — for 12 months post-termination."

CLAUSE C (Liability Cap):
"Total liability shall not exceed fees paid by Client in the 3 months preceding the claim."

CLAUSE D (Auto-Renewal):
"Agreement renews automatically for successive one-year terms unless written notice of non-renewal is given 90 days before the end of the current term."
  • Ask Claude: "Explain each clause in plain English and flag any risks"
  • For Clause A: "Does this mean they can claim my personal side projects?"
  • For Clause C: "If I pay ₹50k/month, what's the maximum I can recover in damages?"
  • For Clause D: "If this starts January 1st, when exactly must I notify them to avoid auto-renewal?"

Clause A: Potentially yes — "whether or not during working hours" is broad. Ask them to add "related to the Company's business" to limit the scope.

Clause B: "Expressed intention to operate" is dangerously vague — almost any market qualifies. The 12-month period is significant. Get legal review on enforceability.

Clause C: If paying ₹50k/month → max recovery = ₹1.5 lakh, regardless of actual damage. For critical services, negotiate for 12 months of fees as the minimum cap.

Clause D: For a January 1 renewal → you must notify by October 3rd. Miss that date = locked in for another full year.

📋 Policy Summarisation

Policy Summary
Document type: [HR Policy / Compliance / Regulatory]
My role: [Employee / Manager / New joiner]

Summarise with:
1. PURPOSE — what is this trying to achieve?
2. WHO IS AFFECTED
3. KEY RULES — 5–8 most important, in plain English
4. WHAT I MUST DO — required actions
5. WHAT I MUST AVOID — explicitly prohibited
6. CONSEQUENCES — what happens if violated
7. EXCEPTIONS — any circumstances with different rules?

Plain English only — no HR or legal jargon.
