✉️ Writing Professional Emails

Universal Email Template
Act as a professional business writer.

Situation: [Who you are, who you're writing to, the relationship]
Purpose: [What this email must achieve]
Key points: [The 2–4 main things the email must say]
Tone: [Formal / Friendly-professional / Firm / Empathetic]
Constraints: [Word limit, phrases to avoid, deadlines]

Write with a subject line. Concise and purposeful.
❌ "Write an email asking my manager for a day off"
✅ Role + specific date + coverage arranged + tone = ready to send
Request Email Prompt
Act as a professional employee writing to their manager.

Situation: I need March 21st off (family commitment). Good relationship with manager Ravi. No critical deadlines that day. Colleague arranged to cover urgent matters.

Purpose: Request the day off without over-explaining.

Key points: Specific date, coverage confirmed, available by phone for urgent matters.

Tone: Professional but relaxed.

Subject line + email. Under 120 words.
⏱ 18 min drafting → 3 min with Claude
⚠️
3 Bad News Mistakes

1. Burying the news in 3 paragraphs  ·  2. Over-apologising  ·  3. No clear next step

Bad News Email Prompt
Act as a senior PM experienced in client communications.

Situation: Software launch delayed 3 weeks — April 1st to April 22nd. Client: Apex Retail. Delay cause: critical security issues found in QA. Strong client relationship.

Purpose: Inform, maintain trust, give a clear next step.

Key points: Lead with the new date directly. Brief honest reason (protecting their data). What we're doing. Offer a call this week.

Tone: Direct, empathetic, confident — not defensive.

Subject line + 3 short paragraphs + CTA. Under 180 words.
⏱ 30 min agonising → 5 min with Claude
❌ "Just following up on my previous email…" (easy to ignore)
✅ Lead with value, reference the specific ask, create mild urgency
Follow-Up Email Prompt
Act as a professional business communicator.

Situation: Sent a proposal to Northstar Analytics 10 days ago. Strong interest shown in meeting, no reply yet. Deal worth ₹8L. Need a response.

Purpose: Get a reply — yes, no, or let's discuss.

Key points: Reference original proposal briefly. Add new value (relevant case study). Mild urgency (finalising Q2 slots this week). Give 3 easy reply options.

Tone: Confident, helpful. Zero desperation.

Short — subject + 3 sentences + bullet options. Under 100 words.
🧠
The "No with a Bridge" Structure

Acknowledge → Decline clearly → Brief reason → Offer an alternative path

Polite Decline Prompt
Act as a professional communicator skilled in tactful messaging.

Situation: Former colleague asked me to be a reference. I can't give them a strong one — mediocre work together. Don't want to damage the relationship.

Purpose: Decline clearly but kindly. Offer a face-saving alternative.

Key points: Decline without being critical. Suggest who might be a better fit. Leave relationship intact.

Tone: Warm, direct, non-judgmental.

Subject + 2 short paragraphs. Under 120 words. No clichés.

✏️ Rewriting Difficult Messages

💡
The Rewrite Technique

Write your raw draft first — even if it's too blunt. Then ask Claude to rewrite it. Your draft has the real substance; Claude fixes the delivery.

😤 Rewrite: You're Frustrated and It Shows ▼
❌ "I've asked THREE times and nothing has changed. This is unacceptable."
✅ "This is the third follow-up. Our team is blocked pending resolution. Can we align on a timeline by EOD today?"
Cool-Down Rewrite Prompt
Rewrite the message below — same urgency and facts, but calm and professional.
Remove emotional language. Make it assertive, not aggressive.
Goal: get action and preserve the relationship.

🤝 Rewrite: Giving Critical Feedback ▼
Constructive Feedback Rewrite
Rewrite this feedback to be:
- Clear and specific (don't sugarcoat the issue)
- Constructive (about the work, not the person)
- Motivating (end with a path forward)
- Professional in tone

Audience: [colleague / junior team member / vendor]

[PASTE YOUR DRAFT HERE]
🚨 Rewrite: Escalating Without Drama ▼
Escalation Email Prompt
Act as a professional escalating a problem to their manager.

Situation: [Issue, who's involved, how long, business impact]

Write an escalation email that:
- States the problem factually (no blame, no drama)
- Explains the business impact
- States what you've already tried
- Asks for a specific decision or intervention

Tone: Calm, factual, solution-oriented.
Format: Subject + 3 paragraphs + clear ask. Under 200 words.
🌐 Rewrite: Wrong Register for the Audience ▼
Register Adjustment Prompt
Rewrite for this audience: [C-suite executive / international client / junior team member]

For executives: More concise. Lead with the ask.
For international clients: Simpler vocabulary. No idioms or jargon.
For junior team members: Warm, encouraging, clear instructions.

Keep all key information. Change only the delivery.

The Bullet Notes → Full Report Technique

Jot bullet notes during the week (10 min). Use Claude to transform them into a polished report (30 sec). Review and adjust (10 min). Total: 20 min instead of 2 hours.

4. Blockers & Risks (honest — what's stuck and why)
5. Next Week's Focus (top 3)
6. RAG Status: Red / Amber / Green — with one sentence justification

Tone: Professional, factual. Written for a 60-second read.

🖥️ Presentation Ideas & Outlines

Presentation Outline
Act as an experienced presentation coach.

Topic: [What the presentation is about]
Audience: [Who they are, what they care about]
Duration: [Minutes including Q&A]
Purpose: [Inform / Persuade / Train / Sell]
Key message: [The one thing they must walk away believing]
Constraints: [Things to include, avoid, sensitivities]

Create a slide-by-slide outline for [N] slides:
- Slide title
- 3 bullet points of content
- Suggested visual
- One key takeaway

Include: an opening line and a closing statement.
5 Opening Hooks
Generate 5 opening hooks for a presentation on [topic] to [audience].

One hook each using:
1. A surprising statistic
2. A provocative question that challenges their assumption
3. A brief relatable scenario (30 seconds)
4. A bold prediction
5. A visual or interactive opener

For each: opening line + 2 sentences bridging to the main topic.
The audience should feel [curious / concerned / motivated] after the opening.
Speaker Notes
Write speaker notes for this slide.

Slide title: [Title]
Bullet points: [List them]
My speaking time for this slide: [e.g. 2 minutes]

Notes must:
- Sound natural when spoken — NOT like written text being read
- Expand on bullets with "why" and context
- Include a transition sentence to the next slide
- Flag one spot to pause for audience reaction

Write talking points and prompts — NOT a word-for-word script.
Q&A Preparation
I'm presenting on [topic] to [audience].

Generate the 8 most likely challenging questions — including the ones I'd prefer not to answer.

For each question:
1. Why they're likely to ask it
2. A confident, honest model answer (2–4 sentences)
3. One thing to avoid saying in response

Focus on questions that challenge my assumptions, data, or recommendations.

Pitch a New Code Review Tool to Leadership

Scenario: Pitch adopting GitHub Copilot for PRs to your VP of Engineering + CPO. 10 minutes. They're technical but skeptical about cost and adoption time.

  • Use the Slide Outline prompt — fill in all fields for this scenario, ask for 8 slides

✅ Model Prompt
Act as a presentation coach specialising in technical pitches to senior leadership.

Topic: Adopting GitHub Copilot for Pull Request reviews across the engineering team
Audience: VP Engineering + CPO — both technical, both cost-conscious, skeptical of adoption overhead
Duration: 10 min (8 presenting, 2 Q&A)
Purpose: Persuade — get approval for a 30-day pilot
Key message: A 30-day pilot costs less than 2 days of developer time and could save 4–6 hrs/developer/week in review overhead
Constraints: Be honest about the learning curve. Don't mention competitors by name.

Create an 8-slide outline with slide title, 3 content bullets, suggested visual, and one key takeaway per slide.
Include an opening line that challenges an assumption and a closing that makes the ask obvious.
