Topic 1 of 10

🌅 Morning Planning with AI

Most professionals start their day reactively — inbox, notifications, whatever fires first. A structured morning briefing with Claude takes under five minutes and sets the frame for everything that follows.

💡
The Daily Briefing Mindset

Treat Claude like a chief of staff who has read your context overnight. You feed it your priorities; it helps you structure, sequence, and prepare for the day ahead.

The Three-Part Morning Briefing

1
Daily Briefing — What matters today?Dump your tasks, meetings, blockers into Claude and get a structured priority view. High-stakes items surface first. Context you need for each meeting is ready.
2
Priority Setting — Where does focus go?Use Claude to apply the Eisenhower matrix or your own criteria to your task list. Decide what you won't do today — that's as important as what you will.
3
Agenda Drafting — How will the day run?Turn priorities into a time-blocked agenda. Claude surfaces prep items for each meeting, buffers for deep work, and flags any scheduling conflicts.
Daily Briefing Prompt
Today is [Day, Date]. Here's what I'm working with:

MEETINGS: [List meetings + times]
TASKS PENDING: [List open tasks]
BLOCKERS: [Anything waiting on others]
DEADLINES THIS WEEK: [Any hard dates]

Act as my chief of staff. Give me:
1. The 3 things that matter most today and why
2. What I should prep before each meeting (2 bullets per meeting)
3. One thing I can defer without consequence
4. Any conflict or risk I should address first thing
Priority Setting Prompt
Here is my full task list for today:
[Paste or describe your tasks]

My role: [Your role]
My current top goal: [What matters most this week]
Time available for deep work: [hours]

Apply the Eisenhower Matrix: Urgent+Important / Important-not-Urgent / 
Urgent-not-Important / Neither.

Then give me:
- My "Do First" list (max 3 items)
- What to schedule for later this week
- What to delegate or drop entirely
- The one task I'm probably avoiding but shouldn't be
Agenda Drafting Prompt
My top priorities today: [from briefing above]
My calendar: [paste rough schedule / meetings]
I work best for deep tasks: [morning / afternoon]
Commute or fixed constraints: [any]

Build me a time-blocked day plan:
- Deep work blocks for my priority tasks
- 10-min prep slots before each meeting
- A protected lunch / break
- A 15-min end-of-day review slot

Flag any overload or unrealistic expectations clearly.
🌅 Exercise 1 — Run Your Morning Briefing

Build Your Real Day Plan with Claude

  • Open a fresh Claude conversation and paste the Daily Briefing prompt with your actual tasks and meetings
  • Then run the Priority Setting prompt on the output to tighten your focus list
  • Finally, use the Agenda Draft prompt to generate a time-blocked plan
  • Compare it to how your day actually ran — what did Claude surface that you hadn't thought of?

⚡ You Can Now…

  • Start every workday with a structured briefing instead of reacting to the inbox
  • Have your priorities, prep items and agenda ready within 5 minutes of sitting down
  • Make a conscious decision each morning about what you won't do today

Topic 3 of 10

🔄 Reviewing, Iterating & Improving AI Outputs

The first Claude output is rarely your best version. The professionals who get the most from AI aren't the ones who accept first drafts — they're the ones who iterate systematically.

❌ Passive Use
  • Accept the first output without review
  • Lightly edit surface-level wording
  • Never tell Claude what was wrong
  • Switch topics without building on the draft
  • Treat Claude as a vending machine
✓ Active Iteration
  • Critique the output before accepting it
  • Tell Claude exactly what missed the mark
  • Ask it to improve a specific part
  • Push for a tighter, stronger version
  • Treat Claude as a collaborator

The Three-Step Iteration Framework

🔄 Review → Critique → Improve
1
Review with Intent

Read the output asking: Does this do the job it was meant to do? Is the tone right? Is anything missing, wrong, or weak? Identify specifically what you want to change — don't just feel it's not right.

2
Give Precise Critique

Tell Claude exactly what didn't work. "The opening is too formal for this audience." "The second paragraph buries the key point." "This misses the fact that we have a tight budget." Vague feedback gets vague revisions.

3
Request Targeted Improvement

Ask Claude to revise only what needs changing — not rewrite everything. "Rewrite just the opening paragraph to be warmer and more direct. Keep everything else." This preserves what worked while fixing what didn't.

Ask Claude to Critique Its Own Output
Review the email / document you just wrote.

Act as a senior editor. Identify:
1. The weakest sentence or paragraph — why?
2. Any point that's vague when it should be specific
3. Any word or phrase that sounds generic rather than my voice
4. Anything a reader might misunderstand or push back on

Then rewrite the output fixing those specific issues.
Don't change what was working — only what you flagged.
Targeted Section Edit
The draft you produced is mostly good but I want to change:

ISSUE: [Describe specifically what's wrong with a section]
SECTION TO FIX: [Quote or describe the part]
WHAT I WANT INSTEAD: [Describe the goal for that section]

Rewrite ONLY that section. Keep all other paragraphs identical.
Show me: OLD VERSION → NEW VERSION side by side.
Tone Adjustment Prompt
Rewrite the following [email / message / paragraph]:
[Paste the content]

Current tone: [e.g., too formal / too casual / too defensive]
Target tone: [e.g., warm but authoritative / direct but respectful]

Audience: [Who is reading this]
My goal with this communication: [What I want them to do or feel]

Keep the same facts and structure. Only shift the tone and voice.
🔄 Exercise 3 — The Three-Pass Iteration

Take a Draft from Good to Excellent

  • Generate a first-draft email or document using a prompt from Topic 2
  • Before editing it yourself, run the Self-Critique prompt — what does Claude flag?
  • Now apply the Targeted Edit prompt for the weakest section it identified
  • Compare your Pass 1 vs Pass 3 output — how much stronger is the final version?

⚡ You Can Now…

  • Systematically improve Claude outputs instead of accepting or abandoning first drafts
  • Use Claude to critique its own work — faster than doing it yourself
  • Get to a polished professional output in 2–3 targeted passes

🛤️ Building Repeatable AI Workflow Habits

The gap between professionals who get marginal value from AI and those who transform their output isn't intelligence — it's habit. Repeatable workflows convert one-off experiments into compounding productivity gains.

⚠️
The Experimentation Trap

Most people try Claude once for a task, get a mediocre result, and stop. The fix isn't a better tool — it's a better habit. Prompts improve with repetition. Context builds with practice.

Four Habit Patterns for Sustained Productivity

📋 The Prompt Library Habit ▼

Every time you write a prompt that works well, save it. Build a personal prompt library — a document or notion page with your best prompts organised by task type (emails, summaries, analysis, code, planning).

Within two weeks of doing this, you'll stop typing prompts from scratch. You'll open your library, paste, fill in the blanks, and go. Time per task drops by 60–70%.

Start today: Create a document called "My Claude Prompt Library" with sections for your most frequent work tasks. Paste your best prompt from this module as the first entry.

🔁 The Trigger-Routine-Reward Loop ▼

Trigger: Pick a consistent moment — first task of the day, every time you open a new doc, every long email you need to write. The trigger is the on-ramp to AI use.

Routine: The standard action you always take at that trigger. For example: "Every time I need to write anything longer than 5 sentences, I open Claude first."

Reward: You finish in a quarter of the time with a better result. The reward is the time and mental energy saved. That positive reinforcement builds the habit automatically.

📂 The Context-Persistence Habit ▼

Claude's most powerful mode isn't single prompts — it's long conversations where context builds. Build the habit of keeping one Claude conversation open per project for the full week. Don't start fresh every time.

At the start of each project thread: paste your role, your goals, the key constraints, and the audience. That context persists across all subsequent prompts in the conversation. You stop re-explaining yourself.

Combine this with Claude Projects (if available in your plan) to make the context permanent across sessions.

📊 The Weekly AI Audit Habit ▼

At the end of each week, spend 10 minutes asking: Which task this week took longer than it should have? Could Claude have helped? What prompts worked well that I should save?

This keeps your workflow improving. After four weekly audits, you will have identified your personal top-10 use cases and have a polished prompt for each one.

⚡ You Can Now…

  • Build a personal prompt library so you stop starting from scratch
  • Identify your personal AI triggers — the moments where Claude should always come first
  • Maintain persistent context so Claude becomes more useful over time, not less

🖥️ Cowork — Extending Claude to Your Desktop

🤝
What is Cowork?

Cowork is a desktop tool that extends Claude's capabilities to your local machine — letting you automate file management, task tracking, and document workflows without writing a single line of code.

What Cowork Does

📁
File Management

Automatically organise, rename, sort, and move files based on rules you describe in plain English. No shell scripts. No manual dragging.

✅
Task Tracking

Connect to your task lists and let Claude surface what's due, overdue, or blocked — updated across your desktop and projects automatically.

📄
Document Workflows

Automate repetitive document creation: generate weekly reports, fill templates from data, convert between formats — all triggered with a prompt.

🔗
Desktop Integration

Works alongside your existing apps — no migration needed. Claude operates on your actual files, folders, and local workflows.

Cowork for Non-Developers

Cowork was designed explicitly so that people without coding experience can automate repetitive desktop tasks. If you can describe a task in plain English, Cowork can help you build a workflow for it.

🚀 Getting Started with Cowork
1
Install Cowork

Download and install from claude.ai. Sign in with your Anthropic account — Cowork is connected to your existing Claude subscription.

2

3
Review, Test, and Activate

Cowork shows you exactly what it will do before running. Confirm, test on one item, then activate. The workflow runs automatically from that point forward.

Team Update / All-Hands Draft

Audience: My team of [size] — [role types]
Period covered: [week / sprint / month]

Key points to include:
- Wins: [list]
- Challenges or blockers: [list]

Channel: [Slack / email / meeting opener]

Status Report — From Raw Notes
Here are my raw project notes for this week:
[Paste your notes / updates / Slack threads]

My stakeholders: [Who reads this report]
Report format required: [RAG / narrative / table]

Generate a status report with:
- Overall RAG status + one-line reason
- Progress against key milestones
- Risks and mitigations
- Decisions needed from stakeholders (if any)

Keep it under one page. Use direct language — no padding.
Onboarding Document / Handbook Section
I need to create an onboarding document for new [role] joiners.
Team: [Team name and purpose]
Key tools they use: [list]
First-week priorities: [list]
Common mistakes new joiners make: [list]

Write a friendly, structured onboarding guide:
- What we do and why it matters (2 paragraphs)
- Your first 5 days (day-by-day)
- Who to go to for what (people + tools)
- Top 3 things that make someone successful here
Performance Feedback Draft
I need to write performance feedback for [role / period].
Strengths I've observed: [list specific examples]
Areas for development: [list specific examples]
Overall trajectory: [on track / exceeding / needs support]

Write structured feedback that is:
- Specific (not generic)
- Balanced (lead with strengths)
- Actionable (development points have a clear "what to do")
- Tone: direct and caring — not harsh, not vague

This will be delivered [in a meeting / in writing].
Keep it to 300–400 words.
