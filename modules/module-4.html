💡 Brainstorming Ideas

❌ "Give me ideas to improve team productivity"
✅ "My team of 8 remote engineers loses most time in unclear requirements and slow PR reviews. Give me 10 specific low-cost ideas — 5 per area. For each: estimate effort and likely impact."
Focused Brainstorm
Act as a creative strategist in [your field].

Challenge: [Specific problem or goal]
Context: [Who you are, team, resources]
Constraints: [Budget / time / tools / what to avoid]

Generate [number] specific, actionable ideas.
For each:
- One-sentence description
- Why it works in this context
- Implementation effort: Low / Medium / High
- Most likely obstacle

Avoid generic ideas — make them specific to my context.

Force different perspectives — surfaces ideas your viewpoint can't generate.

Persona-Based Brainstorm
I need ideas for: [challenge]
Context: [brief situation]

Generate ideas from 4 different personas:
1. A frugal startup founder (no budget)
2. A large enterprise operations manager
3. The end user / customer
4. A contrarian critic of obvious approaches

3 specific ideas per persona.
Mark your personal recommendation with ⭐.

Generate "make it worse" ideas first — then flip each into a solution. Breaks mental blocks.

Reverse Brainstorm
I want to improve: [goal / challenge]

Step 1: 8 specific ways to make this WORSE or fail.
Make them realistic — things that actually happen.

Step 2: Flip each into a corresponding improvement action.

Step 3: Top 3 most actionable flipped ideas for my context:
[Brief context and constraints]

Format: Two-column (Problem Behaviour → Improvement), then top 3.
Categorised Ideas
Challenge: [Problem or opportunity]
Context: [Situation and constraints]

15 ideas in 3 categories:
- QUICK WINS: Implement this week, low effort, immediate impact
- MEDIUM-TERM: 1–4 weeks, moderate effort, solid impact
- STRATEGIC: Longer-term, higher effort, transformational

5 ideas per category. Specific — not generic best practices.
End with: "The one idea I'd prioritise first is X, because..."
💡 Exercise 1 — Brainstorm a Real Challenge

Pick Your Scenario and Run the Focused Brainstorm

  • Engineer: "Sprint velocity dropped 30% after two senior devs left. Generate ideas to recover it within one sprint."
  • Manager: "Team engagement dropped after a restructure. Budget is limited. Generate ideas to rebuild it."
  • Student: "Build a professional network before graduating in 3 months with no work experience."
  • Run the Focused Brainstorm — are any ideas surprising?
  • Then try the Reverse Brainstorm on the same problem
  • Compare: which technique produced more useful ideas?

⚡ You Can Now…

  • Generate 15–20 specific, contextual ideas in under 2 minutes
  • Break mental blocks using the reverse brainstorm technique
  • Get perspectives you'd never generate from your own viewpoint alone

⚖️ Comparing Options

🎯
The Right Question

Don't ask "which is better?" — ask "which is better for me, given my specific criteria?" The criteria are yours. Claude does the analysis.

Options Comparison Table
Options:
A: [Description]  B: [Description]  C: [if applicable]

My context: [Who you are, success criteria, constraints]

Compare across these dimensions:
[List your 4–6 criteria]

For each cell: rating (1–5) + 1-sentence justification.
After the table: recommend the best option for MY context.
Flag any option with a disqualifying weakness.
Weighted Criteria Analysis
Options: [list them]

My criteria and weights (total = 100%):
- [Criterion 1]: [weight]% — [why it matters]
- [Criterion 2]: [weight]% — [why it matters]
- [Criterion 3]: [weight]%
- [Criterion 4]: [weight]%

Score each option per criterion (1–10).
Calculate weighted total. Show in a table.
Recommend, and explain what would tip the decision if scores are close.
Structured Pros / Cons
Options: A: [desc]  B: [desc]
My situation: [context, goals, constraints]

For each option:
PROS: 4–5 genuine advantages (specific to my situation)
CONS: 4–5 genuine disadvantages or risks
HIDDEN RISKS: 2 things that could go wrong non-obviously
BEST CASE: Success in 6 months looks like...
WORST CASE: Failure in 6 months looks like...

Recommendation + the single most important factor driving it.
Scenario Analysis
Options: [list them]
Context: [brief situation]

Analyse each option under 3 scenarios:
BEST CASE: [favourable conditions]
BASE CASE: [most likely conditions]
WORST CASE: [unfavourable conditions]

For each option × scenario:
- What happens? (1–2 sentences)
- How recoverable is a bad outcome? High / Medium / Low

Which option performs best across ALL scenarios?
Which is most robust to uncertainty?

🔍 SWOT Analysis ▼
SWOT Analysis
Act as a senior business strategist.
Analyse: [your plan / idea / decision]
Context: [who you are, goals, constraints]

4–5 specific points per quadrant (no generic clichés):
STRENGTHS — Internal advantages that are genuinely differentiated
WEAKNESSES — Internal limitations honestly acknowledged
OPPORTUNITIES — External conditions you can specifically exploit
THREATS — External risks that could realistically materialise

After the SWOT: 2–3 sentence strategic assessment.
Then: the single most important action — leverage the best opportunity while mitigating the biggest threat.
⚰️ Pre-Mortem — What Will Kill Your Plan? ▼
Pre-Mortem
My plan: [what you're about to commit to]
Context: [team, resources, timeline, dependencies]

Imagine it's 12 months from now. This plan failed completely.

Step 1: 8 specific, realistic reasons why it failed.
Cover: execution, external events, stakeholder issues, technical risks, human factors.

Step 2: Rate each — Probability (H/M/L) × Impact (H/M/L)

Step 3: Top 3 failure modes to mitigate now.
For each: one concrete preventive action to take this week.
😈 Devil's Advocate — Challenge Your Decision ▼
Devil's Advocate
Act as a smart devil's advocate — find genuine blind spots, not just be contrarian.

My decision: [what you've decided]
My reasoning: [why you think it's right]
Context: [relevant background]

Challenge this by:
1. The 3 strongest arguments AGAINST this approach
2. Assumptions I'm making that might be wrong
3. Who would strongly disagree — and why they might be right
4. The scenario where I'd look back and say "I should have done the other thing"

Be direct. Don't soften the critique.
🧱 First Principles — Break the Assumptions ▼
First Principles
Problem: [describe the challenge]
Context: [what's been tried, current constraints]

Apply first principles thinking:
1. What assumptions does everyone make about this problem?
2. Which assumptions, if removed, would change the solution entirely?
3. What is actually true at the most fundamental level?
4. From those fundamentals — what new solutions become possible?

Focus on 2–3 non-obvious directions conventional thinking misses.
🎯 Exercise 3 — Pre-Mortem Your Project

Find What Could Kill Your Current Plan

Use a real project, or: "Launching a new internal tool to replace our current Jira workflow for a team of 12."

  • Run the Pre-Mortem — are any failure modes surprising?
  • For each of the top 3 risks: "What's the minimum action this week to reduce this risk?"
😈 Exercise 4 — Challenge a Decision You've Made

Devil's Advocate a Decision You Feel Confident About

  • Think of a decision you've made recently that you feel confident about
  • Run the Devil's Advocate prompt — does any point genuinely challenge you?
  • Follow up: "Given these critiques, what one change to my plan addresses the most serious concern?"

The best Devil's Advocate responses challenge your specific reasoning — not generic risks. Look for moments where Claude identifies an assumption you hadn't noticed you were making. That's the signal it's working.

If the output feels shallow: "You're being too gentle. What's the single strongest case that this decision is wrong? Don't hedge."

⚡ You Can Now…

  • Apply SWOT, pre-mortem, or first principles to any decision in under 5 minutes
  • Use devil's advocate to find blind spots before you commit
  • Make decisions with documented reasoning — not just gut feel

🔧 Problem Solving

🔧 The Problem Solving Workflow
1
Define the Problem Precisely

Articulate what's happening, who's affected, and what "fixed" looks like

2
Diagnose Root Causes

Use 5 Whys — don't solve symptoms, find what's driving them

3
Generate Solution Options

Multiple approaches at 3 levels: symptomatic, structural, preventive

4
Evaluate and Select

Use comparison frameworks against your real constraints

5
Plan Implementation

Break into specific actions with owners, timelines, success metrics

Problem Definition
Situation: [what's going wrong, who's affected, impact]
Context: [since when, what's been tried]

Help me define the problem precisely:
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT: "The problem is that [who] cannot [what] because [why], resulting in [impact]."
2. SCOPE: What's inside this problem? What's outside it?
3. SUCCESS CRITERIA: What does "fixed" look like specifically?
4. CONSTRAINTS: What limits the solutions?
5. ASSUMPTIONS: What are we assuming that might not be true?

Don't propose solutions yet.
Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys)
Problem: [describe the symptom]
Context: [when it happens, who's affected, what's been tried]

Apply 5 Whys:
Why 1: Why is [problem] happening?
Why 2: Why is [answer 1] happening?
Why 3: Why is [answer 2] happening?
Why 4: Why is [answer 3] happening?
Why 5: Why is [answer 4] happening?

State the root cause in one sentence.
Is this a process problem, a people problem, or a system problem?
3-Level Solution Generation
Root cause: [state it]
Context and constraints: [brief description]

Solutions at 3 levels:
SYMPTOMATIC FIX: Stop the bleeding immediately (days)
STRUCTURAL FIX: Address the root cause (weeks–months)
PREVENTIVE FIX: Stop this class of problem recurring (longer-term)

2–3 options per level.
Mark recommended option per level ⭐ with a brief reason.
End: "The most important thing to do first is X, because Y."
Hypothesis Testing
My hypothesis: [what I believe is the cause or solution]
My reasoning: [evidence / logic behind it]
Context: [the situation]

Test this:
1. EVIDENCE FOR — what supports it?
2. EVIDENCE AGAINST — what contradicts it?
3. ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESES — what else could explain the same symptoms?
4. CHEAPEST TEST — fastest way to validate or disprove?
5. VERDICT — strong, weak, or needs more evidence?

Be honest — tell me if I'm likely wrong.
🔧 Exercise 5 — Solve the PR Review Problem

Full Problem-Solving Workflow

Scenario: Your team's PRs sit open for an average of 3 days. Developers are frustrated. Some are merging without full reviews to hit deadlines.

  • Step 1: Define the problem precisely — write the Problem Statement sentence
  • Step 2: Apply 5 Whys to find the root cause (hint: there may be more than one)
  • Step 3: Use 3-Level Solution Generation on the root cause you found
  • Step 4: "Which solution fits a small team with no budget?"

Problem Statement: "The problem is that developers cannot get timely feedback on PRs because reviewers don't prioritise reviews over their own coding tasks, resulting in 3-day cycle times and developers bypassing the process."

Key insight: The root cause is almost never "developers are lazy" — it's usually a process or incentive design problem. No one was ever told "reviewing PRs is your highest priority task."

📅 Structuring Plans

Project Plan
Act as an experienced project manager.

Project: [Name and description]
Goal: [What success looks like]
Timeline: [Start and end date]
Team: [Who's involved and their roles]
Constraints: [What you cannot compromise on]

Create a structured plan:
1. PHASES: 3–5 phases with clear milestones
2. TASKS: Key tasks per phase (critical path only)
3. RISKS: Top 3 with mitigation strategies
4. DEPENDENCIES: What must happen before what?
5. SUCCESS METRICS: How to measure success per phase

Be realistic — not optimistic.
30-Day Action Plan
Goal: [what I want to achieve in 30 days]
Starting point: [where I am now]
Available time: [hours per day/week]
Constraints: [what limits me]
Priority: [if time runs short, what matters most?]

Week 1: Foundation (set up, learn, clarify)
Week 2: Core work (main activities driving progress)
Week 3: Refinement (iterate and deepen)
Week 4: Completion and review

3–5 specific actions per week with clear done/not-done criteria.
End: "The most critical thing in week 1 is X, because..."
Goal Breakdown
My goal: [describe it]
Timeframe: [when you want it]
Context: [situation, resources, constraints]

Break this down:
1. OUTCOME GOAL: What exactly does success look like? Make it measurable.
2. PROCESS GOALS: 3–5 behaviours/activities that drive progress
3. MILESTONES: 4 checkpoints to track progress
4. FIRST 3 ACTIONS: What to do starting today
5. OBSTACLES: Top 3 likely obstacles and how to pre-empt each

Make this specific enough to start today.
Risk Register
Plan: [brief description]
Context: [team, timeline, key dependencies]

Top 8 risks. For each:
- Risk description (one specific sentence)
- Category: Technical / Resource / External / Process / Stakeholder
- Probability: High / Medium / Low
- Impact if it occurs: High / Medium / Low
- Mitigation: what to do now
- Contingency: what to do if it occurs

Sort by risk score (High+High first). Flag top 3 as priority.

🔗 Exercise 7 — Full Thinking Workflow

Chain All 5 Topics in One Conversation

Scenario: Launch an internal knowledge-base tool. 6 weeks, 2 developers, AWS hosting only.

  • Step 1 — Brainstorm: 10 ideas for MVP features (Focused Brainstorm)
  • Step 2 — Compare: Shortlist 3 tech approaches using Weighted Criteria
  • Step 3 — Decide: Pre-Mortem on the chosen approach
  • Step 4 — Problem-Solve: 5 Whys on the biggest technical risk
  • Step 5 — Plan: 6-week project plan with phases and risk register

Each step uses the previous step's output as input — all within one conversation. Claude maintains context throughout, so you never re-explain your situation. The full workflow takes 30–45 minutes with Claude. Without it: 3 days of meetings and research.
